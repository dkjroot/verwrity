@scene: The last ferry
@summary: Mara returns to Greywater as the last ferry leaves and discovers a message meant for her.

The ferry bell sounded three times before the rope was thrown loose. Mara Vale stood at the stern with one hand on the rail and the other closed around her father's brass compass. The needle had been pointing east all afternoon, away from the river and toward the dark ridge beyond town. Now, as the boat pulled from the landing, it swung sharply north. She watched the little iron roof of Greywater Ferry shrink behind a veil of rain.

She had been away for seven years, long enough for the town to turn her absence into a story. The ferryman had not asked why she was back. He had only passed her a folded scrap of paper, saying that a boy in a red scarf had left it beneath the lantern on the ticket desk. The paper held one sentence in her father's handwriting: Do not let them light the eastern window.

Across the water, the Lantern House showed a single golden square high above the landing. It had been empty since the winter her father disappeared. Mara felt the compass tremble against her palm. On the far bank, someone raised a lantern and covered it twice, as if answering a signal.

@scene: The house above the steps
@summary: Mara enters the abandoned Lantern House and finds evidence that someone has been maintaining it.

The path from the ferry climbed through wet grass and blackthorn. Mara knew every bend, though the river had eaten part of the old stone wall and the town had replaced the lower gate with a chain. At the top stood the Lantern House, its plaster walls silver in the rain. The eastern window still burned.

Elias Wren was waiting beneath the porch. He had been Mara's closest friend before she left, and he now wore the blue coat of the town watch. He looked older than she remembered, with a white scar at his temple and a habit of listening before he spoke. He said he had come to arrest her if the house proved occupied. Then he handed her an iron key.

Inside, dust lay thick on the tables but nowhere else. The floor had been swept in a careful path between the kitchen and the eastern stair. A fresh cup sat beside the cold hearth. In the upstairs room, the glass lantern from the old river beacon had been polished until it shone. Its wick was trimmed, its reservoir full, and a blue thread tied around its handle.

Mara opened her father's desk with the iron key. Behind a false drawer she found a ledger filled with dates, ferry crossings, and names crossed out in red ink. The final entry was yesterday's date. Beside it, her own name had been written twice.

@scene: A map under the floor
@summary: Nadiya arrives with a warning and the group discovers a hidden map beneath the house.

Nadiya Bell came through the back door carrying a sack of bread and a pistol wrapped in oilcloth. She had been Mara's schoolmate, though school had ended early for Nadiya when her mother fell ill. Now she ran the bakery near the river and knew every conversation that passed through the market. She said the town council had sent men to search the house at dawn.

Elias did not trust her. Mara remembered that distrust as something older than either of them, a knot tied during the winter of the great flood. Nadiya set the bread on the table and told them about Tom Arlen, a boy who carried messages between the ferry and the ridge. Tom had seen a lantern burning in the eastern window on nights when no one could have reached the house.

They searched the floorboards until Nadiya noticed that the dust did not settle evenly beneath the desk. Elias lifted the boards with the iron key. Beneath them lay a tin cylinder wrapped in waxed cloth. Inside was a map drawn on sail canvas. It showed the river, the ferry steps, and a second channel that did not exist on any modern chart. The channel ended beneath the Lantern House.

A note pinned to the canvas read: The light is a door only when the river is high. Mara looked toward the eastern window. The flame had gone out, but a thin line of smoke curled upward behind the glass.

@scene: Tom's red scarf
@summary: Tom Arlen is found hiding near the ferry, carrying a key and a story that changes the map.

They found Tom beneath the ferry platform at dusk. His red scarf was caught on a nail, and he was trying not to cough. He had been sleeping in the boathouse since the council men came looking for him. Tom was fourteen, quick with numbers, and proud of knowing which floorboards creaked in every building between the river and the market.

He said the message beneath the lantern had not been meant for Mara at first. Her father had written it for Tom, but Tom could not read the old shorthand. He had carried it to the ferry clerk because Mara's name was the only one he recognized. The boy also had an object wrapped in newspaper: a small brass key with a notch cut through its bow.

Elias asked where he got it. Tom pointed toward the water. On the night before Mara returned, he had seen a woman in a grey coat walk out of the river without wetting her shoes. She had opened a trapdoor beneath the ferry stairs and spoken to someone below. Tom heard the name Orin, then saw the glass lantern flash blue.

Nadiya spread the sailcloth map across a crate. Tom corrected its markings from memory. The hidden channel ran beneath both banks, and the entrance beneath the Lantern House was not the only one. The second entrance lay under the ferry's oldest stone pier. When the river rose, the two openings would connect. The flood was due before midnight.

@scene: The ledger of crossings
@summary: The friends decode the blue ledger and learn what Mara's father was protecting.

The blue ledger had belonged to Mara's father, though its cover was newer than the paper inside. They carried it to Nadiya's bakery, where the ovens gave them enough heat to dry the map. Nadiya brewed bitter coffee and arranged the ledger beside the brass compass. The compass needle pointed toward the same page every time Mara set it down.

Elias read the dates aloud. Each entry recorded a crossing made during a flood, but the names were not passengers. They were objects: a bell, a mirror, a crown of river reeds, a sealed letter, and finally the glass lantern. Beside each object was a single word: carried, hidden, returned, refused, waiting.

Mara recognized her father's shorthand in the margins. He had believed the river remembered every bargain made beside it. The hidden channel was an old watercourse built by the first settlers, a place where the river could be diverted for one hour at the highest tide. The council had used it to move contraband during wars, then erased it from the town records. Her father had discovered that the council planned to open it again and drown the lower market.

Nadiya found the missing page tucked inside the ledger's spine. It named the council's leader: Orin Venn, the woman Tom had seen at the pier. Mara felt the compass settle. Its needle was no longer pointing north. It pointed down.

@scene: The eastern window
@summary: Orin Venn confronts the group at the Lantern House and reveals why the eastern light matters.

Orin Venn arrived at the Lantern House with four council men and a lantern of her own. She was broad-shouldered, grey-haired, and dressed like someone who expected doors to open before she touched them. She called the blue ledger a collection of superstitions. Elias answered that the town records called it evidence.

Orin said Mara's father had not disappeared. He had chosen the river over the town. Years earlier, he had entered the hidden channel to stop a flood and never found the way back. The eastern window was not a door to another place, she said, but a signal to the mechanism beneath the house. When the glass lantern burned blue, the old sluice opened. When it burned gold, it closed.

Mara asked why Orin had been lighting it. Orin replied that the lower market had been built over a fault in the riverbed. The next flood would destroy it unless the sluice was opened first. The council intended to sacrifice the abandoned quarter so the ridge villages would survive. Elias called it murder. Orin called it arithmetic.

While they argued, Tom slipped behind the stair and used his notched key on a narrow brass plate. The floor opened with a sigh. Cold air rose from below, carrying the smell of deep water. On the underside of the trapdoor, Mara saw her father's final message: Choose the bank that has a future.

@scene: Beneath Greywater
@summary: The four friends enter the hidden channel and find the mechanism that controls the flood.

They descended with the glass lantern between them. Orin followed after the council men refused to remain above. The stairs ended in a tunnel where the walls were lined with old ferry tokens. Water moved beneath the stones, not flowing forward but circling as if it had forgotten which direction was down. The brass compass pulled Mara toward a wheel of black iron set into the wall.

Nadiya found four sockets in the wheel. The objects named in the ledger fit them: the bell, the mirror, the sealed letter, and the brass key. The glass lantern belonged in the centre. Each object carried a different piece of the town's history. The mechanism had not been built to obey a single keeper. It required a witness from the ferry, a keeper of records, a member of the watch, and someone who had lost a house to the river.

Elias understood first. The four roles were not titles but choices. Tom was the witness because he had seen the hidden entrance. Nadiya was the keeper because she had preserved the old stories. Elias was the watchman because he could name the danger. Mara was the one who had lost a house, even though the house had been her father's and not hers.

They placed the objects together. The wheel turned. Far above, the eastern window flashed blue, and the river began to rise through the tunnel.

@scene: The light we choose
@summary: Mara and her friends redirect the flood and decide what to do with the truth they uncovered.

The mechanism gave them one hour. Orin wanted to open the sluice toward the lower market, arguing that the ridge villages held more people. Elias found a second lever hidden behind the wheel. It would divide the water between the market and the ridge, saving neither completely but preventing either from being erased. Orin called it cowardice. Nadiya called it a chance for the town to choose together.

Mara remembered her father's warning and understood the missing part. He had not asked her to obey the ledger. He had asked her to refuse the habit of letting one person decide whose home counted. She pulled the second lever. Tom rang the old bell, and its sound travelled through the tunnel, across the ferry, and into every street where people were waking to the flood.

The water split. The market lost its cellars and three empty warehouses. The ridge road collapsed, but the houses above it held. By dawn, Greywater was muddy, frightened, and still standing. Orin surrendered the council seal to Elias. Nadiya carried the blue ledger to the square and read the names aloud.

Mara climbed to the eastern window. The glass lantern burned gold now. In the morning light, the compass pointed nowhere special. She tied a blue thread around its handle and left the door open.

@wiki: Mara Vale
@tags: character, protagonist, ferry clerk
@aliases: Mara, Miss Vale, Wave Strider

Mara Vale is a former ferry clerk who returns to Greywater after seven years away. Her father disappeared while investigating the hidden river channel beneath the town. Mara carries his brass compass and initially believes it will lead her to answers. Over the story she learns that the compass responds to choices and pressure rather than pointing toward a fixed destiny.

@wiki: Elias Wren
@tags: character, town watch, ally

Elias Wren serves in the Greywater town watch. He and Mara were close as children, but he has kept his distance since her father's disappearance. Elias is cautious, observant, and uncomfortable with decisions that cannot be enforced by a written rule. He becomes the person willing to name the council's plan as a deliberate sacrifice.

@wiki: Nadiya Bell
@tags: character, baker, historian

Nadiya Bell runs the bakery near the ferry market and remembers stories that the official records omit. She is practical, quick to anger, and generous with food. Her knowledge of local families and flood customs lets the group understand that the hidden mechanism was designed for shared responsibility rather than private control.

@wiki: Tom Arlen
@tags: character, messenger, witness

Tom Arlen is a fourteen-year-old messenger who knows the shortcuts, loose boards, and forgotten doors around Greywater. He wears a red scarf and sleeps in the ferry boathouse while hiding from the council search. Tom's observations correct the old map and make him the first person to see Orin Venn using the hidden entrance.

@wiki: Greywater Ferry
@tags: location, river, town

Greywater Ferry is the crossing between the lower market and the ridge road. Its oldest stone pier hides the lower entrance to the forgotten river channel. The ferry is both a practical route and the place where the town's public stories are exchanged.

@wiki: Lantern House
@tags: location, river beacon, mystery

The Lantern House stands above the ferry steps. It was once maintained by Mara's father as a river beacon, but it has been officially empty since his disappearance. Its eastern window contains the signal lantern and conceals the upper entrance to the flood mechanism.

@wiki: The brass compass
@tags: object, inheritance, clue

Mara's brass compass belonged to her father. Its needle does not consistently follow magnetic north. It reacts to the hidden river mechanism, especially when someone nearby is making a consequential choice. Mara begins by treating it as a tool and ends by keeping it as a reminder that direction is not the same as certainty.

@wiki: The glass lantern
@tags: object, signal, mechanism

The glass lantern is the old beacon from the Lantern House. A gold flame closes the hidden sluice; a blue flame opens it. The lantern is not an independent source of power, but it makes the state of the mechanism visible from the river and the town below.

@wiki: The blue ledger
@tags: object, evidence, history

The blue ledger records flood crossings, hidden objects, and the decisions made by previous keepers of the river channel. Its final pages identify Orin Venn's plan to sacrifice the lower market. Nadiya preserves it as evidence after the flood rather than allowing the council to return it to the locked archive.

@wiki: The iron key
@tags: object, access, inheritance

The iron key opens Mara's father's desk and the concealed floor panel in the Lantern House. Elias gives it to Mara before the council search. It represents official access to the house, but the group discovers that access alone cannot operate the hidden mechanism.
